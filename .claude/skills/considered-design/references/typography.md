# Typography

## Part 1 — Ten Named Pairings

Each entry: pairing, Google Fonts URL, character, when to use, when not to.

---

### 1. **Civic Charter** — Heritage & Civic

**Heading:** Source Serif 4 (700/600)
**Body:** Source Sans 3 (400/500)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Serif+4:opsz,wght@8..60,400;8..60,600;8..60,700&family=Source+Sans+3:wght@400;500;600&display=swap`

**Character:** Sober, accountable, US-government-adjacent trust. Same designer family means guaranteed harmony — Serif for the institutional voice (acts, charters, councils), Sans for the operational UI (forms, nav, captions).

**Use:** Civic portals, municipal sites, regulatory bodies, tribunals, public records.

**Avoid when:** You want warmth, craft, or editorial flourish.

---

### 2. **Long Read** — Editorial / Magazine

**Heading:** Fraunces (700, opsz 144)
**Body:** Inter Tight (400/500)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Fraunces:opsz,wght@9..144,400;9..144,500;9..144,700&family=Inter+Tight:wght@400;500;600&display=swap`

**Character:** Fraunces is a 2024-era revival with optical sizing — it morphs from a stately display to a sturdy text face. Inter Tight is the tightened, more editorial sibling of Inter. The pair signals "long-form seriousness with personality" — think a serious policy magazine or a public-interest journal.

**Use:** Foundation reports, long-form journalism, museum editorial, think-tank journals.

**Avoid when:** UI density matters more than reading rhythm.

---

### 3. **Quiet Authority** — Warm Institutional (NGO, Hospital, University)

**Heading:** Newsreader (600, opsz 40)
**Body:** Source Sans 3 (400/500)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Newsreader:opsz,wght@6..72,400;6..72,500;6..72,600&family=Source+Sans+3:wght@400;500;600&display=swap`

**Character:** Newsreader was purpose-built for screen reading with oldstyle numerals and a humanist warmth. It signals care without sentimentality. The sans body keeps the UI legible. Perfect for healthcare systems, universities, social-sector NGOs that want to be both rigorous and humane.

**Use:** Hospital systems, universities, NGOs, care organisations, palliative services.

---

### 4. **Ledger Modern** — Modern / Clean SaaS / Public Service

**Heading:** IBM Plex Sans (600)
**Body:** IBM Plex Sans (400/500) **+ IBM Plex Mono** for code, IDs, data tags

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=IBM+Plex+Sans:wght@400;500;600;700&family=IBM+Plex+Mono:wght@400;500&display=swap`

**Character:** Single-family discipline. IBM Plex Sans is engineered for data-dense UIs (forms, tables, dashboards). Plex Mono adds a third voice for technical context: timestamps, IDs, references, code, JSON. This pairing says "operational system" — calmer than Geist, more institutional than Inter.

**Use:** Permit systems, civic apps, case-management dashboards, data catalogues.

**Avoid when:** You need warmth or editorial voice in the prose.

---

### 5. **Scholarly Folio** — Heritage / Academic

**Heading:** EB Garamond (600/700)
**Body:** Inter Tight (400/500)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=EB+Garamond:wght@400;500;600;700&family=Inter+Tight:wght@400;500;600&display=swap`

**Character:** EB Garamond carries centuries of scholarly typography. Pairing it with Inter Tight — not Inter — keeps the modern surface intact. The combination says "this institution has a history worth showing." Headings get gravitas; body stays unassuming.

**Use:** University faculties, classical archives, court histories, heritage trusts, theological bodies.

---

### 6. **Field Notebook** — Cultural / Craft / Heritage NGO

**Heading:** Fraunces (500, opsz 9) — used SMALL and tight
**Body:** Newsreader (400/500) — actually carries the long-form voice

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Fraunces:opsz,wght@9..144,400;9..144,500&family=Newsreader:opsz,wght@6..72,400;6..72,500;6..72,600&display=swap`

**Character:** Two serif families used as a hierarchy of voices — Fraunces at small sizes for labels and UI marks (where its quirks make marks memorable), Newsreader for the body (where its readability shines). No sans serif. This is the "we make things" tone: craft schools, museums of folk art, documentation projects, oral-history archives.

**Use:** Museum micro-sites, craft institutions, oral history projects, design schools.

### 7. **Premium Acrylic** — Premium / Sophisticated (Foundations, Patron Sites)

**Heading:** Marcellus (400)
**Body:** Karla (400/500)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Marcellus&family=Karla:wght@400;500;600&display=swap`

**Character:** Marcellus is a single-weight Roman display — quiet, expensive, never shouts. Karla is a grotesque sans with just enough personality to be warm without being soft. The single-weight heading is a *statement*: it says we do not need to underline. Used right, it reads more expensive than Playfair.

**Use:** Donor pages, patron sections of foundations, gala sites, art-foundation micro-sites.

**Avoid when:** You need a clear 600/700 hierarchy for body emphasis.

### 8. **Town Square** — Friendly / Approachable Civic

**Heading:** Fraunces (600, opsz 144) — used BIG and ROUND
**Body:** Nunito (400/600)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Fraunces:opsz,wght@9..144,500;9..144,600;9..144,700&family=Nunito:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap`

**Character:** Fraunces at large sizes rounds into a friendly, almost-circular terminal. Nunito is the most legible of the humanist sans-serifs at small sizes and has rounded shoulders that match. The pair feels welcoming without becoming a kindergarten site — used by civic onboarding, public-library campaigns, neighborhood-services portals.

**Use:** Public libraries, community centres, civic onboarding, voter education.

### 9. **Court Record** — Editorial / Magazine (Alternative)

**Heading:** Spectral (600, opsz 36)
**Body:** Karla (400/500)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Spectral:wght@400;500;600;700&family=Karla:wght@400;500;600&display=swap`

**Character:** Spectral was designed for long-form digital reading with a humanist edge. Karla softens the sans with curved terminals. The pairing reads like an investigative publication — confident but not austere. Suitable for independent reporting outfits, policy commentary, public-interest journalism.

### 10. **Studio Workshop** — Cultural / Craft (Alternative)

**Heading:** Tenor Sans (400)
**Body:** Work Sans (400/500)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Tenor+Sans&family=Work+Sans:wght@400;500;600&display=swap`

**Character:** Tenor Sans is single-weight, geometric, and quietly elegant — designed for fashion editorial. Work Sans is a Swiss grotesque with proper proportions. The pair says atelier: design studios, small museums, contemporary craft projects, architecture practices.

**Use:** Architecture firms, design practices, contemporary craft, small museum sites.

## Part 2 — Type Scale

Use a **modular scale**. For institutional sites, the right anchor is **Major Third (1.250)** — generous enough for hierarchy, restrained enough not to fragment the page.

```
base     = 16px (1.000) → body, captions, labels
md       = 18px (1.125) → large body, lead paragraphs
lg       = 20px (1.250) → small UI headlines, card titles
xl       = 25px (1.563) → section headers (h3)
2xl      = 31px (1.953) → page section headers (h2)
3xl      = 39px (2.441) → page headers (h1, desktop)
4xl      = 49px (3.052) → display, hero (use sparingly)
5xl      = 61px (3.815) → only on landing pages, max one per page
```

For larger, more editorial scales, use **Perfect Fourth (1.333)** — appropriate for long-form publications.

**Rules:**

- **Never use more than 4 sizes on a single page.** Anchor: h1, h2, body, caption.
- **Lead paragraphs (`.lead`)** = base × 1.125 (18px) at 1.6 line-height, body colour at 80% opacity.
- **Captions** = 14px, body weight, +1% letter-spacing.

## Part 3 — Rules

### Weight

- **Headings:** 600, occasionally 700 for h1. Never 800+ — weights above 700 look cheap.
- **Body:** 400 default. 500 only for inline emphasis (`<strong>`), nav, and table headers.
- **Avoid mixing more than three weights per page.** The most common mistake is loading 300/400/500/600/700/800 and using three of them on every paragraph.

### Line-height

- **Display (≥40px):** 1.05–1.15
- **h1/h2 (28–40px):** 1.15–1.25
- **h3/section headers (20–28px):** 1.30–1.40
- **Body (16–18px):** 1.55–1.65
- **Small body / captions (≤14px):** 1.50

### Letter-spacing

- **Display (≥49px):** -0.020em (tighter)
- **h1 (32–40px):** -0.015em
- **h2/h3:** -0.010em
- **Body:** 0 (default)
- **Captions / labels / uppercase:** +0.020em to +0.040em
- **All-caps small labels:** +0.080em, all-lowercase tracking is the wrong answer

### Paragraph measure (line length)

- **60–75 characters** per line, including spaces. Aim for 68ch.
- Implement as `max-width: 38rem` (about 65ch on most body fonts) on prose containers.
- For narrow mobile, never let a body line exceed 38ch.
- **Never** use full-bleed body text at 1280px+.

### Paragraph rhythm

- **Paragraph spacing:** 1.0em between paragraphs in long-form (set margin-top equal to 1em).
- **No first-line indent** for institutional sites — paragraph spacing is clearer.
- **Section breaks:** A single centered asterisk, or 1em of vertical space, never a horizontal rule on editorial sites.

### Colours for type

- **Body text:** never pure black. Use `#1A1A1A` or your grey-9.
- **Secondary text (meta, captions):** 60% opacity of body colour, never a coloured grey.
- **Hyperlinks:** colour only, no underline, **unless** the link is inline within prose — then underline by default.
- **Contrast:** WCAG AA minimum. Body text 4.5:1 against background. Large text 3:1.

## Part 4 — Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it's wrong | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Inter for everything** | Reads as default. Looks like every AI mockup. | Pick a serif for headings (Fraunces, Source Serif 4, Newsreader, EB Garamond). |
| **Roboto fallback** | Roboto is to 2026 what Arial was to 2006. | Sensible system stack: `ui-sans-serif, system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", Roboto, sans-serif`. |
| **Identical weight across the page** (everything 400 except a single 600 button) | No hierarchy. Eyes don't know where to land. | Three weights minimum: 400 body, 500 nav/UI, 600 headings. |
| **All-lowercase body text** | Display conceit for portfolios. Reduces legibility. | Sentence case. Title Case only for proper titles. |
| **Playfair Display on every luxury site** | Mass-market-template. | Use Marcellus, EB Garamond, Cormorant, or — best — Fraunces for premium display. |
| **Tight letter-spacing on body text** | Reduces readability under 16px. | Letter-spacing 0 on body. Tighten only above 32px. |
| **Centred body paragraphs of more than two lines** | Creates uneven rivers, slows reading. | Left-align body. Centre only short labels, single sentences, captions. |
| **Display font at body size** | Display fonts lose character below 20px. | Display fonts start at 28px+ only. |
| **Three different sans-serifs on one page** | Visual schizophrenia. | One sans family + one serif or mono. Never two sans faces. |
| **Loading 8 weights of a font and using 2** | Performance cost with no design return. | Load only the weights + styles you use. Use `font-display: swap` and preload the body face. |
| **Drop caps on institutional sites** | Reads as school newsletter or boutique hotel. | Reserve for editorial articles. Never in nav, headers, or civic copy. |
| **Letter-spaced ALL CAPS for body emphasis** | All-caps is for labels, not body. | Use italics (true italic) or weight 500–600. |

## Part 5 — Bilingual Pairings (Devanagari + Latin)

Devanagari text needs different proportions than Latin. Most bilingual sites fail because the heading face was never designed for Devanagari, so it falls back to a system face that does not match.

**Rule of thumb:** pair a *family that ships both scripts*, or a Latin heading face with a Devanagari body face that visually rhymes with it.

### Recommended bilingual pairings

#### B1. **Civic Bilingual** — Government / Public Records

- **Heading (Latin):** Source Serif 4
- **Body (Latin):** Source Sans 3
- **Devanagari companion:** Noto Serif Devanagari (headings) + Noto Sans Devanagari (body)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Serif+4:opsz,wght@8..60,400;8..60,600;8..60,700&family=Source+Sans+3:wght@400;500;600&family=Noto+Serif+Devanagari:wght@400;600;700&family=Noto+Sans+Devanagari:wght@400;500;600&display=swap`

**Why it works:** Noto Serif/Sans Devanagari were *designed* to match Source Serif / Source Sans proportions. Same x-height grid, same colour. The Devanagari does not feel like a fallback.

#### B2. **Editorial Bilingual** — Magazine / Long-form

- **Heading (Latin):** Fraunces
- **Body (Latin):** Newsreader
- **Devanagari companion:** Mukta (heading + body)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Fraunces:opsz,wght@9..144,500;9..144,600;9..144,700&family=Newsreader:opsz,wght@6..72,400;6..72,500;6..72,600&family=Mukta:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap`

**Why it works:** Mukta is humanist, slightly warm, has open counters. Visually rhymes with Newsreader's roundness. Good for Hindi editorial.

#### B3. **Cultural Heritage Bilingual** — Museum / Cultural Institution

- **Heading (Latin):** EB Garamond
- **Body (Latin):** EB Garamond (400, used at two weights)
- **Devanagari companion:** Rozha One (display headings only) + Tiro Devanagari Hindi (body)

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=EB+Garamond:wght@400;500;600;700&family=Rozha+One&family=Tiro+Devanagari+Hindi&display=swap`

**Why it works:** Rozha One is a single-weight Devanagari display face with serious display authority — it rhymes with EB Garamond's gravitas. Tiro Devanagari is a serif companion designed for body. Together: a museum-grade bilingual system.

#### B4. **Modern Bilingual** — SaaS / Civic Tech

- **Heading (Latin):** IBM Plex Sans
- **Body (Latin):** IBM Plex Sans
- **Devanagari companion:** IBM Plex Sans Devanagari + IBM Plex Mono Devanagari

`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=IBM+Plex+Sans:wght@400;500;600;700&family=IBM+Plex+Sans+Devanagari:wght@400;500;600;700&family=IBM+Plex+Mono:wght@400;500&family=IBM+Plex+Mono+Devanagari:wght@400;500&display=swap`

**Why it works:** The entire IBM Plex family ships Devanagari. Same designer, same engineering rigour. Best-in-class for data-dense bilingual systems.

### Bilingual rules

1. **Never mix-and-match Latin and Devanagari from unrelated families.** The fallback will betray you at 14px body.
2. **Set Devanagari line-height 0.05 higher than Latin.** Devanagari has ascenders for vowel marks that Latin does not — `line-height: 1.65` for body, not 1.55.
3. **Devanagari body weight is 400 by default;** do not bold Devanagari at the same weight as Latin bold, because Devanagari carries inherent visual weight.
4. **Use `<bdi>` and `lang="hi"` markup** so the browser selects the correct glyph variants.
5. **Test mixed-script paragraphs at 14px and 16px.** The Devanagari will pull visual weight; balance with 1–2% more breathing room between mixed-script paragraphs.
6. **Hindi numerals:** use the same numerals as the rest of your system. Do not mix Devanagari digits (०१२) and Arabic digits (012) in the same table.

| Warm / institutional (NGO, hospital, university) | **Quiet Authority** (Newsreader + Source Sans 3) | Rigorous and humane |
| Modern / clean (SaaS, public service) | **Ledger Modern** (IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Mono) | Operational, data-dense |

| Friendly / approachable | **Town Square** (Fraunces + Nunito) | Welcoming without softness |

## Part 7 — Loading Discipline

A typography system fails at the network level before the screen level. Final rules:

1. **Self-host for production.** Google Fonts CDN is fine for prototypes, but for institutional sites serving millions, self-host the WOFF2 files.
2. **Preload the body face and one heading face** with `<link rel="preload" as="font" type="font/woff2" crossorigin>`.
3. **Variable fonts only.** Load one file per family, not eight static weights.
4. **`font-display: swap`** — never `block`. Render text immediately with the fallback, swap when ready.
5. **Variable axes:** load `wght` always; load `opsz` only for Fraunces, Newsreader, Source Serif 4, Spectral — faces that change character with size.

A site that follows the modular scale, the loading discipline, and avoids Inter-everything will read as **considered**, not as AI-generated — even when the AI wrote the HTML.
