# Copy & Voice

This skill file itself is written by someone who has read too many "Welcome to the future of X" landing pages. Treat it that way.

---

## 1. Tone Guidance

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| `Use Inter, 16px / 1.55.` | `You might consider using a clean sans-serif font.` |
| `Avoid background #F8FAFC on body.` | `Light, airy backgrounds work well for modern websites.` |
| `State opinions.` | `Some designers prefer X, while others prefer Y.` |
| `Tables for comparisons, bullets for steps, prose for principles.` | `Three paragraphs that say the same thing twice.` |
| `Name the file. Name the line.` | `Update the navigation component accordingly.` |

Imperatives, not hedges. No "you could", no "one approach is", no "it depends". If something is debatable, say *when* it's debatable — don't default-hedge everything.

Tables where you compare two or more things. Bullets for steps. Prose only when you're explaining a principle that wouldn't survive being chopped up. If a paragraph can become a bullet, make it a bullet.

Hex codes, font names, component names. `text-base text-slate-900`, not "the body text". `leading-[1.55]`, not "comfortable line height". If a reader can't copy the value into their codebase, the instruction failed.

## 2. Copy That Actually Lands

### Voice principles

1. **Specific beats poetic.** "Class 6 to 12, 480 students" beats "a vibrant learning community of young minds".
2. **Honest beats impressive.** "Six teachers, three rooms, one library" beats "world-class faculty and infrastructure".
3. **Active voice, present tense, short verbs.** "We teach", "We publish the timetable in August", "We post results on the notice board". Not "students are nurtured", not "excellence is delivered".
4. **Numbers carry units.** "₹1,200 per month", "40 children per class", "by 14 November 2026". A bare number is a vibes number.
5. **Cut the verb "to be" when you can.** "Admissions open 1 October" not "Admissions are now open". "Results out" not "Results have been announced".
6. **One fact per sentence.** If a sentence has two facts, it has two sentences.
7. **No jargon unless the audience uses it.** A school for parents of Class 1 kids should not say "pedagogical framework". A B2B SaaS can say "API".

### Headline formulas that work

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Plain noun phrase | `N.H.S. Bagodar. A school.` |
| Place + what | `Bagodar, Jharkhand — Class 6 to 12, Hindi medium.` |
| Concrete date | `Admissions 2026–27. Last date 14 November.` |
| Honest constraint | `One building. Six teachers. Forty years.` |
| Question | `How is your child this term?` |
| Offer in plain words | `Free dental check-up, second Saturday every month.` |
| Verb-led | `Read the syllabus. See last year's results.` |

A good headline answers at least one of: *what is this, who is it for, what can I do, when does it happen.* If it answers none, rewrite.

### Body copy patterns

- **Lead with the date.** Indian institutional readers scan for dates, fees, deadlines. Put them in the first sentence of the section.
- **Bullet the constraints.** `Tuesdays and Thursdays · 9 am to 1 pm · Bring your child's Aadhaar card · No appointment needed`.
- **Name the human.** `Speak to the principal: Shri R. K. Singh, 06586-XXXXXX`. Not "our leadership team".
- **Quote the document.** If the school has a rule, paste the rule. "Rule 4(b) of the KVS admission guidelines: ..." beats "in line with prevailing regulations".
- **End with a verb.** A paragraph that ends on a noun is decoration. A paragraph that ends on "Apply by 14 November" or "Read the full syllabus below" works.

### CTA patterns

The verbs that survive: **Apply, Read, Download, See, Call, Visit, Book, Get, Find, Watch**.

| Dead CTAs (cut) | Living CTAs (use) |
|---|---|
| `Get started` | `Apply by 14 November` |
| `Learn more` | `Read the syllabus` |
| `Discover` | `See last year's results` |
| `Explore` | `Download the fee structure (PDF)` |
| `Sign up` | `Book a 20-minute call` |
| `Join us` | `Walk in on a Tuesday. 9 am to 1 pm.` |
| `Contact us` | `Call the office: 06586-XXXXXX` |
| `Submit` | `Send the form by 10 November` |
| `Find out more` | `Read what we teach, class by class` |
| `Continue` | `Go to Class 6 syllabus` |

Rule of thumb: a CTA should answer *what happens next, and when*. If your CTA can't be turned into a sentence with a date or a file or a phone number, it's wrong.

### Voice for institutional context (school, civic, hospital, panchayat)

- Respectful. Never breathless. No exclamation marks unless you mean them.
- "We" is fine. "We" doesn't need to become "Team Bagodar" or "The N.H.S. Family".
- Honour names fully. `Smt. Prabha Devi, Headmistress`. Not `Prabha`, not `Ms. P. Devi`.
- Write in the language the institution actually uses internally. If the office says "Shiksha Vibhag", the site can say "Shiksha Vibhag".
- Don't translate warmth into cheerfulness. Warmth is `We will call you back the same day`. Cheerfulness is `We're super excited to help!`.

### Indian English voice

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| `We'll get back to you the same day.` | `We shall revert at the earliest.` |
| `The office is closed on Sundays.` | `The office shall remain closed on Sundays.` |
| `₹1,200 per month` | `Rs. 1200 p.m.` (use the symbol, not "Rs") |
| `1,00,000 (one lakh)` | `100,000` (institutional copy in India should lakh-crore) |
| `Please find the form below.` | `Please find attached herewith the form.` |
| `Respected Sir / Madam` (in formal letter pages) | `Dear Esteemed Patron` |
| `14 November 2026` | `11/14/2026` (ambiguous in Indian contexts — spell it) |
| `Class 6` | `Grade 6` or `6th standard` depending on which the school actually uses |

Keep Indian English natural, not anglicized. The danger is the *opposite* of sounding AI — it is sounding like a 2008 corporate email from a bank. Strip "kindly do the needful", strip "at the earliest", strip "the same". Keep "revert" only if the institution still uses it.

## 4. Phrase-Shape Anti-Patterns

These are templates — the specific words can change, the *shape* is the smell.

- **"[Verb] your [noun] with our [noun]"** — "Transform your learning with our platform". Kill.
- **"Welcome to the next generation of [X]"** — anything starting with "Welcome to the" is on watch.
- **"We are committed to..."** — institutional code for "we wrote a sentence to fill space". Replace with a fact.
- **"Proud to announce..."** — only use if the thing is actually unusual for that institution. A new water tank is news. A new logo is not.
- **"We are delighted to inform you..."** — formal Indian-English phrasing that aged into corporate-speak. Use "We are pleased to inform you" if you must, or just the fact.
- **"In the realm of..."** — never.
- **Superlatives with no number.** "The best school in the district" with no evidence is a sentence that loses trust. If you claim it, prove it: "Topper, Class 12 board exam, 2024 — 94%".
- **"And much more!"** — finishing a list with this means the list was filler.
- **Three adjectives in a row.** "Innovative, holistic, student-centred approach" is one adjective short of a parody. Pick one, or none.

## 5. Anti-AI Headline Patterns — What Good Looks Like

### Five families of headline

**Plain statement.** State what the place is, in plain words. No metaphor.

- `N.H.S. Bagodar. A school, not a campus.`
- `A government higher secondary school in Bagodar Block, Giridih district.`
- `Anganwadi Kendra, Bagodar. Open 9 am to 1 pm.`

**Honest constraint.** Tell the reader something specific they couldn't have guessed — a number, a rule, a limit.

- `Six teachers. 480 students. One building.`
- `No hostel. Yes to mid-day meal.`
- `Class 6 to 12. Hindi medium. Co-ed.`

**Concrete date.** Indian institutional readers scan for dates. Give them dates.

- `Admissions 2026–27. Open 1 October. Close 14 November.`
- `Parent–teacher meeting: 22 September, 10 am.`
- `Results out: 25 May 2026, 9 am, school notice board.`

**Question.** A real question, not a rhetorical flourish. The reader should be able to answer it.

- `How is your child this term?`
- `When was the last time the building got a coat of paint?`
- `What does Class 6 actually study in a year?`

**Verb-led action.** Imperative, second-person, specific.

- `Read the syllabus, term by term.`
- `See last year's Class 12 results.`
- `Walk in on a Tuesday. We'll show you around.`

### Headline anti-patterns (specific shapes, not just phrases)

| Anti-pattern shape | Example of failure | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `[Adjective] [noun] for the [adjective] [noun]` | `Empowering education for the leaders of tomorrow` | `N.H.S. Bagodar. A school.` |
| `Welcome to [X], where [cliché]` | `Welcome to N.H.S., where dreams take flight` | `Welcome to N.H.S. Bagodar. Office hours: 9 to 4.` |
| `[Verb] your [noun]` | `Transform your child's future` | `Admissions open 1 October. Last date 14 November.` |
| `Discover the [superlative] [noun]` | `Discover the best of Bagodar` | `Read what we teach, class by class.` |
| `The [adjective] [noun] of [noun]` | `The future of education, today` | `Class 6 syllabus. Download (PDF, 240 KB).` |
| `[Noun] reimagined` | `Learning, reimagined.` | Delete the page. |

## 6. Body Copy Anti-Patterns

- **Paragraphs that start with "At N.H.S., we believe..."** — every institutional site has one of these. Cut it. Replace with a fact.
- **"Our mission is to..."** — a mission statement that is not on a wall plaque is decoration. Either move it to a plaque page or kill it.
- **Three paragraphs of context before the first date or number.** Indian institutional readers want the date. Lead with it.
- **"We strive to..."** — anything with "strive" is a confession of failure. Cut.
- **Decorative subheadings.** `## Excellence` over a paragraph that doesn't define excellence. Subheadings should let a reader skip ahead and still get the facts.

---

## 7. What to Do Instead

End every section with one of:

- A date (`14 November 2026`)
- A number (`₹1,200`, `480 students`, `40 minutes`)
- A file (`Download the syllabus, PDF`)
- A name (`Shri R. K. Singh, Headmaster, 06586-XXXXXX`)
- A place (`Room 4, Ground Floor`)

If the section ends on a noun that isn't any of those, rewrite the section.
